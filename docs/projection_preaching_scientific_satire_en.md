## 正文（保留已确认原文）

“Tell me the difference between projection and preaching and I will give you my P-card number for free.”

The auditorium became quiet. Someone had introduced an uncontrolled variable: a reader.

“Which projection?” asked the moderator. “An axonal projection? A statistical projection? Psychological projection?”

“The one where you put your interpretation inside my experience, then congratulate yourself on discovering it.”

The lecturer advanced to the next slide.

**Slide 1:** Neurons communicate.  
**Slide 2:** Neural circuits contribute to emotion.  
**Slide 3:** Therefore, let me explain what your suffering means.

The transition from Slide 2 to Slide 3 was exceptionally smooth. Unfortunately, the mechanism responsible was PowerPoint.

“I admire the experiment,” the reader said. “Now show me the experiment that authorizes the sermon.”

The lecturer explained that human experience was complex.

“Excellent. Please retain that sentence when discussing mine.”

A senior colleague intervened. Surely an author of such distinction deserved the reader’s trust.

“Certainly. Which claim are we trusting, and what observation would make us stop?”

This question was referred to the book club.

The reader tried again.

“A clinical story can generate a hypothesis. A moving paragraph can make me care. But where did the interpretation acquire immunity from correction? Was that in the supplementary material?”

The moderator suggested that the reader might be uncomfortable with uncertainty.

“I am comfortable with uncertainty. I become uncomfortable when it appears in the methods and disappears from the conclusions.”

At last, a graduate student offered a distinction:

“An interpretation becomes preaching when disagreement is treated as a defect in the listener rather than a possible problem with the interpretation.”

The reader reached for his wallet.

The lecturer added that resistance itself could be revealing.

The wallet closed.
